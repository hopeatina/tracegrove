## What changes on screen

### Conversation, the baseline (all three episodes)
- Two-camera interview: a wide two-shot plus a medium single on each speaker. The Stoute episode is in a bright, daylight living room with big windows and cream chairs. The Jacobs and Dell episodes are in dark, moody offices or studios, with a red painting behind Jacobs.
- One persistent overlay: a small "PRESENTED BY ramp ⟋" wordmark in the top-right corner, white and low opacity. This is the presenting-sponsor bug, and it is the only permanent sponsor element. There are no lower thirds or captions during conversation.

### Format A: older episodes (Jacobs, Dell). The B&W placard.
- A hard cut from the interview to a single locked-off product shot: a white card in a black wooden stand, sitting on a table. It is shot in black-and-white with very shallow depth of field, heavy bokeh and a vignette. The card shows the sponsor's logo in black. The Eight Sleep and Function cards also print the vanity URL in tiny type (`EIGHTSLEEP.COM/SENRA`, `FUNCTIONHEALTH.COM/SENRA`).
- The shot holds for the whole read, from 30 s up to about 2 minutes. The only motion is a slow push-in or drift and shifting background bokeh. Senra is off camera and the read is voice-over only.
- No text overlays, no B-roll, no music tag. The read ends with a hard cut back to the interview.
- The effect: it reads like a "title card" chapter break, and the placard sits in the same visual world (monochrome, tactile, a physical object) as the show itself.

### Format B: newer episode (Stoute). Each sponsor's own branded motion spot.
Each read is a fully produced ad in the sponsor's own visual system, timed to Senra's voice-over. Kinetic type often types out his script word by word. There is a hard cut in from the conversation, and a cut or fade back out. Recurring devices:
- **Kinetic typography of the script**: one phrase at a time, centered, with the key word in the brand color. Examples: "A religious dedication to controlling costs", "by thinking about business / from first principles instead of *formulas*", "recognize A Players", "*Your AI* needs to know".
- **Founder or exec cutaways with a logo lockup**: a portrait (often a desaturated or stage photo) with the company logo beside it. Ramp has Notion, Eventbrite and Webflow. Deel has Shopify, DoorDash and ElevenLabs. HubSpot has Brian Halligan with his quote typed out in orange.
- **Testimonial quote typing on screen** next to the speaker: the ElevenLabs founder for Deel, Halligan for HubSpot.
- **Product-UI mock cards**: simplified, animated interface fragments, never real screenshots. Ramp shows expense cards ("TECH STARTUP $24.0m → $22.8m") and Your business vs Competitor revenue cards. AppLovin shows "Set the goal: ROAS/CPP" and "Set daily budget $15,000 · Live". Deel shows worker profile cards. HubSpot shows a contact card and "Newest Inbound from David Reeves… asked for a price 3 weeks ago".
- **Big-number hero stats** with confetti or particles: "Revenue Increased $280m" (Ramp), "$362m → $415m With AppLovin", "10,231,536" view counter, "1 billion+ potential new customers", "Trusted by over 40,000 businesses."
- **Simple charts**: bar charts ("Ramp customers grow revenue 3.2x faster…", 16% highlighted with a hand-drawn arrow) and a sales-history bar chart (HubSpot).
- **Staged B-roll of Senra**: reading *Zero to One* on a couch, which opens the AppLovin read, overlaid with blue kinetic type. The Deel read uses a B&W shot of two men at a round table.
- **Culture or reference inserts**: a Google search for "valuable private company" and a "Starlink Satellite $400,000" line item in the Ramp spot, a hand-written $50,000,000 check in the Deel spot, and floating game icons (UNO, Tetris, Candy Crush) in the AppLovin spot.
- **The same closing beat in every spot**: a search bar types the vanity URL (`ramp.com|`, `applovin|`, `deel.com/senra|`, `hubspot.com|`) and a cursor clicks it, then the logo resolves on a brand-color field.
- **Palette shift per sponsor**: Ramp uses light grey/off-white with acid-lime highlights and a pale sky-blue gradient. AppLovin uses deep navy-to-electric-blue gradients with a magenta/red "LIVE" moment. Deel uses lavender/white with saturated purple. HubSpot alternates warm orange gradients and a near-black frame with serif type.
- **Back-to-back reads**: Ramp runs straight into AppLovin. The AppLovin spot opens on Senra B-roll and a white "ramp" logo card, so the two spots are bridged without returning to the interview.
- **Music**: the captions tag `[music]` throughout all four Stoute reads and in none of the six older reads. The new spots carry a music bed; the old placard reads appear to be dry voice-over.

### Takeaways for recreating the approach
1. The old format is near-zero-cost and on-brand: one physical object, monochrome, shallow focus, and the host's voice carrying everything.
2. The new format turns the host's script into the edit: every sentence gets one visual idea (a word, a face plus logo, a UI card, a number), and every spot ends on the URL being typed.
3. Sponsor brand worlds are fully adopted inside the read, while the show's own world (the room, the corner bug) disappears for its duration.
